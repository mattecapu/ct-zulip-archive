Why in semigroups satisfying xx=xxx=xxx=x it holds that
xyzyx=xyxzxyxxyzyx=xyxzxyxxyzyx=xyxzxyx Use no advanced theorem, just rewriting rules like AA→AAA\to AAA→A.

Amar Hadzihasanovic (Jul 03 2026 at 19:16):

It does not hold in general bands.
It does hold in regular bands, which satisfy xyxzx=xyzxxyxzx = xyzxxyxzx=xyzx, by the equational chain
(xyxzx)yx=[x(yzx]y)x=x[yz(yxy]x)=xyz[yx](xyxzx)yx = [x(yzx]y)x = x[yz(yxy]x) = xyz[yx](xyxzx)yx=[x(yzx]y)x=x[yz(yxy]x)=xyz[yx]
where round brackets denote the part next rewritten and square brackets the one that's been rewritten; the first two equations by regularity, the third by idempotence.

Amar Hadzihasanovic (Jul 03 2026 at 19:41):

If I understand correctly, if www is a string of elements of XXX (called "variables"),

  • c(w)c(w)c(w) is "the set of all variables appearing in www",
  • σ(w)\sigma(w)σ(w) is the "variable that appears last in www",
  • s(w)s(w)s(w) is the “prefix of www before the first appearance of σ(w)\sigma(w)σ(w)”

So if c(w)={x}c(w) = \{x\}c(w)={x} it means that xxx is the only variable appearing in www, that is, w=x⋯xw = x\cdots xw=x⋯x; so xxx is (trivially) the “last variable”; and because it appears in the first position, there is no “prefix before the appearance of xxx”, so s(w)s(w)s(w) is the empty string.
