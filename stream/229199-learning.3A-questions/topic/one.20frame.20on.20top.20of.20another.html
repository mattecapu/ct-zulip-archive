Topic: one frame on top of another

Morgan Rogers (he/him) (Sep 08 2026 at 12:37):

I've just come up with a construction that takes two frames F,F′F,F'F,F′ and constructs a new frame by identifying the top of FFF with the bottom of F′F'F′ (everything in F′F'F′ is above everything in FFF). The "obvious" joins and meets seem to distribute just fine.

Has anyone encountered this before? Perhaps on spaces? It's a construction that seems reasonable from the algebraic/frame point of view but counterintuitive from the topological point of view.

Chris Grossack (she/they) (Sep 08 2026 at 15:56):

This is a well known operation in the theory of posets/lattices, called the "ordinal sum" or the "ordered sum" or the "linear sum". See, eg, Section 1.24 of my edition of Davey and Priestley's "Introduction to Lattices and Order".

It's definitely "well known" that the linear sum of two distributive lattices is distributive, and people definitely study this (though I'm not sure of any references off the top of my head). I'm not sure of anybody using this notion for frames (and I can't find anything on quickly looking around for it either), but lattice-theoretically it's a very natural thing to consider.

Roy Ferguson (Sep 10 2026 at 11:30):

Sorry I am late to the conversation and thank you @Graham Manuell for sharing! We call this operation "stacking of spaces" and arrived at a special case of it by transporting stacking of preorders along the specialisation equivalence. But this operation works out for Top as well. All these operations are examples of monoidal sum structures (in the sense of Zurab Janelidze), which have the property that a certain nice class of morphisms called the c-reflecting morphisms are all extensive (in the sense Michael Hoefnagel and Emma Theart). Curiously, the usual coproduct and stacking of spaces are the only monoidal sum structures on Top where this is the case (see Section 4.5).

Vincent Moreau (Sep 10 2026 at 17:37):

This discussion is fascinating! And just to add to the caveat, although the ordinal sum of posets has a monoidal unit, its restriction to frames does not as the unit would have to be empty, while the stacking of spaces is a legit monoidal struture with unit

Roy Ferguson (Sep 11 2026 at 06:42):

@Vincent Moreau yes! This was a frustration of ours when looking for sum structures in categories of relational structures bounded above and below. Monoidal sum structures always have unit the initial object, and so stacking/ordinal sum is not a candidate for such categories. But, if we "transport" stacking along the functor Top->Frm, I think this operation actually gives us a monoidal product structure, where the unit is terminal.

Another thing I didn't mention regarding Artin gluings is that stacking and coproduct in Top (the sufficiently extensive ones) are two extremes of Artin gluings, where for coproduct you glue along the map that sends everything to the top, while for stacking you glue along the map that only sends top to top and everything else to bottom.
