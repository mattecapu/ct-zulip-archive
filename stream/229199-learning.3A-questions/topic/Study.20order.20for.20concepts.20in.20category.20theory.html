John Baez (Sep 19 2026 at 06:49):

I seem to recall that book says nothing about monoidal categories; that's a distinct and more modern strand of category theory (starting in 1963 rather than 1945-ca 1955). For that you might try Noson Yanofsky's book on monoidal categories, very expository and packed with examples. But that can come later, if you want. Leinster's book is good for the basic stuff. If Leinster is too hard, you can try Cheng's The Joy of Abstraction, which does categories, functors and natural transformations, up to the Yoneda lemma, with lots of important examples, and lots of the associated ways of thinking spelled out.
