Nathanael Arkor (May 29 2026 at 14:57):

There are various ways to arrive at the definition of lax monoidal functor, but a particularly pertinent way is to see them as monoids in a certain multicategory. If A\mathbf AA and B\mathbf BB are monoidal categories, then [A,B][\mathbf A, \mathbf B][A,B] has a natural (convolution) multicategory structure, where the multimorphisms F1,…,Fn→GF_1, \ldots, F_n \to GF1​,…,Fn​→G are families F1(A1)⊗⋯⊗Fn(An)→G(A1⊗⋯⊗An)F_1(A_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes F_n(A_n) \to G(A_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes A_n)F1​(A1​)⊗⋯⊗Fn​(An​)→G(A1​⊗⋯⊗An​), natural in each variable. A monoid in this multicategory is precisely a lax monoidal functor from A\mathbf AA to B\mathbf BB.

Nathanael Arkor (May 29 2026 at 14:58):

Why do I say this is pertinent? Well, enriched categories can also be seen as monoids in a certain monoidal category (namely, of V\mathscr VV-matrices), which explains where the laws for enriched categories come from.

Nathanael Arkor (May 29 2026 at 14:59):

More generally, enriched categories, enriched functors, and enriched natural transformations are precisely monads, monad morphisms, and monad transformations in the (virtual) double category of V\mathscr VV-matrices. (This is a principle that applies generally for "category-like structures".)

Nathanael Arkor (May 29 2026 at 15:00):

Any time you seem to have unitality and associativity constraints, there's a good chance what you're looking at is a monoid in some monoidal category or multicategory.

Mike Shulman (Jul 30 2026 at 02:22):

Coming to this a little late, but I find that I get more intuition for the notion of lax monoidal functor from the observation that a lax monoidal functor between two monoidal categories is precisely a functor between their underlying multicategories. Here the notion of "functor of multicategories" is the obvious/unavoidable thing that maps objects to objects and multimorphisms to multimorphisms, preserving domains, codomains, identities, and composition.

Mike Shulman (Jul 30 2026 at 06:27):

Another way to explain or justify the correctness of the notion of lax monoidal functor is that it is a lax morphism of algebras for the 2-monad whose algebras are monoidal categories. Of course then you have to justify the latter instead, but I think this is easier because (1) you only have to do it once, rather than separately justifying a notion of "lax morphism" for all the different kinds of structured category in the world, and (2) in the abstract setting it's more obviously correct: there's only one square you can write down that obviously needs a 2-cell in it, and then there's only one cube you can write down involving that square that should obviously commute.

Noam Zeilberger (Jul 31 2026 at 07:58):

Also worth mentioning another characterization of exponentiable multicategories (which I remember seeing mentioned in Ea's talk) as so-called "malleable" multicategories. The definition of malleable (found in Mario Román's thesis) may be considered as a special case of the notion of Conduché fibration of multicategories: a multicategory MMM is malleable just in case the unique functor M→1M \to \mathbf{1}M→1 to the terminal multicategory 1\mathbf{1}1 (with one object and one operation of every arity) is a Conduché fibration. Since Conduché fibrations of multicategories E→BE \to BE→B are in one-to-one correspondence with pseudofunctors B→ProfB \to \mathbf{Prof}B→Prof into the 2-multicategory of small categories and multi-profunctors, every malleable multicategory MMM gives rise to a pseudofunctor 1→Prof\mathbf{1} \to \mathbf{Prof}1→Prof, hence a (unbiased) pseudomonoid in Prof\mathbf{Prof}Prof = promonoidal category.

Nathanael Arkor (Jul 31 2026 at 08:13):

Claudio Pisani (Aug 01 2026 at 13:45):

Perhaps it is worth mentioning also that in the unbiased approach, wherein a symmetric multicategory is a double functor p:M→Pbp:\mathbb M \to \mathbb P\rm bp:M→Pb, a multicategory is exponentiable iff the loose part of ppp is an ordinary Conduché fibration.
Indeed, the loose part of M\mathbb MM is formed by the general operations α\alphaα of the multicategory (parametrized by the functions pαp\alphapα), which are families of elementary operations (those for which pαp\alphapα has a terminal codomain), so that we get the "malleability" condition.

In particular, since Conduché fibrations include fibrations,

  1. representable (aka monoidal) symmetric multicategories are exponentiable because they are those for which the loose part of ppp is an opfibration;
  2. cocartesian (aka sequential) multicategories are exponentiable because the loose part of ppp is a fibration; for instance, if C▹\cal C_\trianglerightC▹​ is the cocartesian multicategory on C\cal CC, then D▹C▹=(DC)▹\cal D_\triangleright ^{\cal C_\triangleright} = (\cal D^{\cal C})_\triangleright D▹C▹​​=(DC)▹​, while SetC▹\rm Set^{\cal C_\triangleright}SetC▹​ has functors F:C→SetF:\cal C\to \rm SetF:C→Set as objects, and arrows Fi→GF_i \to GFi​→G are natural transformations (ΠiFi)Δ→G(\Pi_i F_i) \Delta \to G(Πi​Fi​)Δ→G.
