Topic: Categorified localic Tychonoff theorem

Quentin Schroeder (Oct 29 2026 at 18:04):

In their paper PREFRAME PRESENTATIONS PRESENT by Johnstone and Vickers the authors study the notion of a preframes. These are meet semi-lattices which admit directed joins preserved by the binary meet operation in each variable. One of the results they present is a very elegant proof of the "localic Tychonoff theorem", which states that (potentially infinite) products of compact locales are compact. Here compact means that the top element of the frame is irreducible by directed joins.

My question is whether there is a known generalization of this results for toposes. It is unclear to me how to generalize their proof strategy but I'd be very interested to hear if other people have attempted such a thing!

What I mean concretely is whether there is a statement resembling:
"Arbitrary products of compact toposes are compact", where compact can take different meanings, for example that the terminal object of the topos EEE is:

  • Compact in the sense that global sections (E(1,−)E(1, -)E(1,−)) preserves filtered colimits (the nlab calls this "strongly compact")
  • Compact in the sense that gloabal sections (E(1,−)E(1, -)E(1,−)) preserve directed joins of subterminal objects (the nlab calls this "compact")
