Topic: Coends, Day convolution, and profunctors

Jana Nickel (Oct 26 2026 at 19:24):

I have two questions concerning Day convolution and profunctors.

  1. Let M\mathcal{M}M be a monoidal category. We write ⊗D\otimes_D⊗D​ for the Day convolution monoidal product on the category of presheaves Mop→Set\mathcal{M}^{\textup{op}}\to\textup{Set}Mop→Set. Then for any preshaves FFF, GGG and HHH, natural transformations F⊗DG⇒HF\otimes_D G\Rightarrow HF⊗D​G⇒H are in one-to-one correspondence with natural transformations F(−1)×G(−2)⇒H(−1⊗−2)F(-_1)\times G(-_2)\Rightarrow H(-_1\otimes -_2)F(−1​)×G(−2​)⇒H(−1​⊗−2​). (Cf. Proposition B.4 in Gavranović, for instance.) Does this also hold for strict 2-natural transformations if M\mathcal{M}M is a monoidal 2-category and FFF, GGG and HHH are strict 2-functors of shape Mop→Cat\mathcal{M}^{\textup{op}}\to\textup{Cat}Mop→Cat?
  2. Writing Prof\textup{Prof}Prof for the pseudo double category of small profunctors (whose objects are small categories, whose vertical arrows are ordinary functors, whose horizontal arrows A→B\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}A→B are profunctors, i.e., functors Bop×A→Set\mathcal{B}^{\textup{op}}\times \mathcal{A}\to \textup{Set}Bop×A→Set, and whose 2-cells are appropriate natural transformations), then horizontal composition in Prof\textup{Prof}Prof is given by a coend formula, see Shulman, for example. Can this coend be described more explicitly? In particular, does there exists an explicit characterization of the composition constraint of lax double functors from a pseudo double category into Prof\textup{Prof}Prof?

Matteo Capucci (he/him) (Oct 27 2026 at 08:52):

Jana Nickel said:

  1. Does this also hold for strict 2-natural transformations if M\mathcal{M}M is a monoidal 2-category and FFF, GGG and HHH are strict 2-functors of shape Mop→Cat\mathcal{M}^{\textup{op}}\to\textup{Cat}Mop→Cat?

yes, because strict 2-stuff is Cat-enriched stuff, so you can use enriched coends and enriched extranaturality there (in general dinaturality doesn't make quite sense in the enriched context, but Cat is a tame enough base that I guess in that case it would work anyway). see §4.3 of Coend calculus.

Matteo Capucci (he/him) (Oct 27 2026 at 08:56):

Jana Nickel said:

  1. Can this coend be described more explicitly?

uhm this one is hard to answer because it depends on what you mean by explicitly. if you unravel the definition of coend, you get a pretty good description already IMO: a proarrow p:a⇝c∈F⊙G(a,c)p:a \rightsquigarrow c \in F \odot G(a,c)p:a⇝c∈F⊙G(a,c) is an equivalence class of pairs a⇝pb⇝qca \overset{p}\rightsquigarrow b \overset{q}\rightsquigarrow ca⇝pb⇝qc (ppp from FFF and qqq from GGG) under a 'tensor-like' equivalence relation: (p⋅h,q′)∼(p,h⋅q′)(p \cdot h, q') \sim (p, h \cdot q')(p⋅h,q′)∼(p,h⋅q′) where h:b→b′h:b \to b'h:b→b′ is any map in the middle category BBB, and ⋅\cdot⋅ denotes the profunctorial action

Matteo Capucci (he/him) (Oct 27 2026 at 08:57):

this is completely analogous to the equivalence that describes the tensor of two bimodules: pairs of elements up to the equivalence that allows you to 'slide' a scalar from the right of one to the left and vice versa
