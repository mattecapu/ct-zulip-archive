Stream: learning: questions

Topic: What kinds of structures can a functor preserve?

Ruby Khondaker (she/her) (Nov 23 2026 at 17:57):

If I list examples of what I've seen, then we have:

  • Monomorphisms, Epimorphisms
  • Limits, Colimits
  • Weighted Limits, Weighted Colimits
  • Weak Kan Extensions, Pointwise Kan Extensions

I'm not as familiar with Kan Lifts, but I assume there is a notion of preservation for these too.

I've been trying to figure out if these are instances of a common concept:

  • The property of being a monomorphism can be phrased as a particular diagram being a pullback, so one can fold preservation of monos/epis into preservation of a particular limit/colimit.
  • Pointwise kan extensions can be viewed as a type of weighted (co)limit, but weak kan extensions generally aren't. Indeed, one of the equivalent definitions of pointwise kan extensions are those kan extensions "preserved by all representables" (this has to be interpreted carefully for left vs right extensions). Absoluteness of kan extensions also comes to mind.

There are also structures like "commutative diagrams" and "isomorphisms" which all functors preserve - I suppose what I'm focusing on are structures that may not be preserved by a functor.

Initially I thought the answer might be "universal element", since that seems to be a common thread behind these structures, all being instances of universal properties. But this is a bit too general!

Namely, if we have some presheaf PPP on a category C\mathcal{C}C for which we have a universal element u∈P(x)u \in P(x)u∈P(x), and some functor F ⁣:C→DF \colon \mathcal{C} \to \mathcal{D}F:C→D, it's not entirely clear how one would "apply" FFF to uuu. Of course, there are ways to transport PPP along FFF to define a presheaf on D\mathcal{D}D - the natural choice is via left kan extension, using cocompleteness of the presheaf category. But this sends representable functors to representable functors and is functorial, which would mean that FFF automatically preserves uuu (meaning, sends it to another universal element). We could instead try the right kan extension, but then the result of applying FFF to uuu can't easily be interpreted as a candidate universal element for some presheaf on D\mathcal{D}D.

So presheaves are a bit too general - there's something special about the universal properties associated to (weighted) limits/colimits and kan extensions that allows one to make sense of their preservation, and this doesn't seem to make sense for arbitrary universal properties. But I've not been able to distill what precisely that special quality is...

I'd also be interested in analogs of the question above for reflection, lifting and creation of structure.

James Deikun (Nov 24 2026 at 12:08):

This still doesn't make "preserving power objects" a special case of "preserving Kan extensions" though -- as can be seen by the fact that the Kan extension presenting an adjoint is absolute and thus preserved by any functor.

James Deikun (Nov 24 2026 at 12:13):

In general though I think a functor can "preserve", and perhaps "lift" and "create", any kind of stuff/structure/property that is 2-monadic (or pseudomonadic?) over Cat.

Ruby Khondaker (she/her) (Nov 24 2026 at 12:17):

Oh! I don’t know much about the theory of 2-monads, but could you perhaps outline how to extract a notion of preservation from such a 2-monad?

James Deikun (Nov 24 2026 at 12:26):

It's basically a weakening of the usual way homomorphisms preserve stuff in algebra--the preserving functor commutes with the structure functors up to unique isomorphism. So for example, for all appropriate diagrams DDD, the "designated" limit cone of FDFDFD is uniquely isomorphic to FFF composed with the "designated" limit cone of DDD.

James Deikun (Nov 24 2026 at 12:36):

(There's also a stricter "on the nose" notion of preservation, but this one is usually the one you want, especially for something specified by universal properties so it is unique up to unique iso if it exists.)
