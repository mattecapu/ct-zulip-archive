Topic: For loops and the yoneda lemma

Alex Kreitzberg (Nov 24 2026 at 21:36):

A friend shared a Haskell idiom:

for :: Functor f => f a -> ( a -> b ) -> f b
for fa g = fmap g fa

For example, the expression for [1, 2, 3] square has the value [1, 4, 9]. This is a "for each expression", and it's relatively common in software.

It occured to me the yoneda lemma amounts to saying the first arrow in the above definition is an isomorphism

Fa≅hom(y(a),F)F a \cong \mathrm{hom}( y(a), F )Fa≅hom(y(a),F)

Has anyone noticed this before? I'm surprised I haven't seen this connection pointed out to me yet.

It seems "readily intuitionable", like the expression

for[0110]\mathrm{for} \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}for[01​10​]

Could stand for the natural transformation that flips matricies with two rows.

Anybody seen this explored before?

Matteo Capucci (he/him) (Nov 25 2026 at 13:24):

Alex Kreitzberg said:

I guess can you expand on what you mean by "yoneda is about functoriality of functors"? I usually think of yoneda as being about representable functors.

a presheaf on C is a left C-module, functoriality is the 'C-action'. Yoneda is the observation that a representable is a free left C-module on a generator of the form a:1→C0a:1 \to C_0a:1→C0​, thus a map of modules ya→Fya \to Fya→F is the same thing as a map a→∣F∣a \to |F|a→∣F∣ , where FFF is just the assignment on objects of FFF, and such a map is trivial on all b∈C0b \in C_0b∈C0​ except on aaa, where it picks out the elements of FaFaFa.
the free-forgetful adjunction which underlies this principle works because FFF is functorial & the maps we consider between functors are natural.
