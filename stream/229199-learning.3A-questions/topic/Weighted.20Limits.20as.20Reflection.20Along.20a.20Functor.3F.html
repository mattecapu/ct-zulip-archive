Topic: Weighted Limits as Reflection Along a Functor?

Ruby Khondaker (she/her) (Nov 15 2026 at 15:31):

One can view an ordinary limit for a diagram D:J→CD : \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{C}D:J→C as a coreflection of it along the constant functor Δ:C→[J,C]\Delta : \mathcal{C} \to [\mathcal{J}, \mathcal{C}]Δ:C→[J,C]. Is there a similar characterisation of weighted limits?

I know that in Set one can view a weighted limit as an ordinary limit by taking a suitable category of elements. But this doesn’t work in the enriched setting.

I guess some corollaries that would happen if this were true - if all weighted limits of a given shape (and weight?) exist, we get some kind of adjunction.

Nathanael Arkor (Nov 16 2026 at 08:53):

For a class of weights Ψ\PsiΨ, you can consider the inclusion of an enriched category C\mathbf CC into its free completion under Ψ\PsiΨ-weighted limits C↪Ψ(C)\mathbf C \hookrightarrow \Psi(\mathbf C)C↪Ψ(C). This has a right adjoint iff C\mathbf CC admits Ψ\PsiΨ-weighted limits.

This isn't exactly the same as situation with adjoints into categories of functors in the unenriched setting, but plays a similar role (and in particular satisfies your requirement that the existence of limits can be seen as an adjointness condition).

Jason Brown (Nov 16 2026 at 11:15):

Hi @Ruby Khondaker (she/her)

Maybe something which looks more like the result from ordinary category theory which you've described is that if your V\mathcal{V}V-category K\mathcal{K}K has copowers, then for a given weight W:J→VW : \mathcal{J} \to \mathcal{V}W:J→V you can form the functor K→[J,K]\mathcal{K} \to [\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}]K→[J,K] given by k↦W−⊙kk \mapsto W_- \odot kk↦W−​⊙k. A right adjoint to this functor computes limits weighted by WWW, i.e. is given by {W,−}:[J,K]→K\{W,-\} : [\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}] \to \mathcal{K}{W,−}:[J,K]→K. This is more formally an "adjunction with a parameter" W∈[J,V]W \in [\mathcal{J},\mathcal{V}]W∈[J,V]. In the case where V\mathcal{V}V is Set\mathsf{Set}Set, taking WWW to be constant at 1∈Set1 \in \mathsf{Set}1∈Set recovers the usual ordinary-category adjunction you describe.
