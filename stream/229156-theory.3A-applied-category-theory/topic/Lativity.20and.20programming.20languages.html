Topic: Lativity and programming languages

PP (Nov 20 2026 at 06:40):

I would like to explore the possibilities of applying lative logic to the development of new programming languages. What advantages would a lative design offer compared to the traditional approach?
Reference: The Fundamentals of Lative Logic

Ryan Wisnesky (Nov 20 2026 at 18:27):

To the extent lative logic is about exploring the differences in small but foundational changes to traditional logics, such as where negation "goes", we see some of that in CQL, where users can work around restrictions in a lack of negation at the formula level by axiomatizing their own boolean type and using boolean terms. In such a setting, questions that were formerly about consistency now become related to conservativity (do the new booleans let us prove anything new), and we have to deal with model elements such as non-standard booleans. Their slides touch on all this in Horn clause logic with a data-driven example, but CQL goes to "regular" logic. http://categoricaldata.net
