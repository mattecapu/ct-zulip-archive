Martin Brandenburg (Sep 02 2026 at 15:40):

Release v1.19.0 adds a technical example of a cocomplete category without equalizers (the free cocompletion of a suitable locally small category). Deciding all of its properties involved quite a lot of work. In this context, free cocompletions and small presheaves have also been studied in general. I was quite amazed to see that this example alone has witnessed 73 new combinations (!) of properties of the form "p and not q", or 153 when duals are taken into account.

Moreover, I have started to break down the property of being regular into its constituent properties by adding the properties of having kernel pairs and of having coequalizers of kernel pairs (and their duals). The PR for the property of having pullback-stable regular epimorphisms is already in progress.

The goal behind that is to have all constituents of a property in the database also recorded in the database. That is, if a property P is defined by "A and B and C are satisfied", we should try to add A, B, and C separately and record the equivalence P ⇔ A + B + C. Deciding A, B, and C for all categories in the database then gives a more detailed understanding of P, and in many cases, multiple proofs can be combined into one.

Furthermore, I realized that it would be better to distinguish between "natural number objects" and "parametrized natural number objects". Previously, only the latter was recorded, but without the "parametrized" prefix in its name, which was potentially confusing. Now, both properties are available in CatDat. The connection to distributivity has also been made more precise.

Finally, someone asked via the suggestion form to add concrete categories (or rather, concretizable categories, since for me and the nLab, "concrete" refers to extra structure, not a property). So this property has been added as well and decided for all categories in the database. In fact, only three categories in the database are not concretizable. This property had been on the list for quite a while, and I was hesitant to add it because I didn't see much value in it. But apparently, this was just my ignorance. After adding it, I learned about Isbell's condition for concretizability, which I find very interesting, applied it to the technical example mentioned at the beginning, and was thereby able to remove the proofs that no generating or cogenerating sets exist, since these now follow as corollaries of non-concretizability.

Having said this, I still do not know (cf. MSE) a single example of a concretizable and cocomplete category without equalizers, or, more or less equivalently, a practical example of a cocomplete category without equalizers.

Martin Brandenburg (Sep 10 2026 at 22:23):

Release v1.20.0 adds several categories that are less common, but satisfy interesting property combinations that had not been witnessed before (e.g. f.g. projective modules over the ring of dual numbers, finite sets with even cardinality, posets without isolated points, and many more). In fact, the number of unwitnessed property combinations of categories of the form "p and not q" went down from 886 to 495.
There are now 112 categories in the database. In total, there are 195 categorical structures along with 23996 proofs of their properties (most of which are automated). Such statistics are now shown on the homepage, which has been redesigned completely.
Since categories in CatDat are not assumed to be locally small, it makes sense to drop this assumption also from preadditive and hence abelian categories. Following this change, several non-locally small abelian categories have been added, such as the functor category [On, Ab] ("long transfinite sequences of abelian groups"), which is an example of a Grothendieck abelian category that does not have a cogenerator. You might be familiar with the theorem that every Grothendieck abelian category has a cogenerator, but this is only true in the locally small case.
Even worse, there are Grothendieck abelian categories that are not complete (hence, not locally presentable), i.e. that do not have products. I have found an example for this combination which is very pathological and probably never comes up in practice, namely the category of vector spaces over a large field (such as surreal numbers) that have a small basis (details).
From all the new categories, all properties have been decided. Unfortunately (see here), some properties are still undecided for much more common categories, and I have started to think again about the difficult question if CMon has effective cocongruences (MO/511792).
Daniel Schepler has added total and cototal categories. There we also thought about allowing non-locally small categories, but decided against it because of the resulting technical issues and since MO/513986 did not receive any attention.

Martin Brandenburg (Sep 26 2026 at 19:52):

Release v1.21.0 follows the same objective as the previous one; among other things, it adds 14 new categories that satisfy interesting property combinations. Just to name 5 of them:

  • large families of sets which are mostly singletons (is cartesian closed but has no generating collection),
  • directed graphs with finite components (has a subobject classifier but not coequalizers),
  • connected sequences of sets (has a subobject classifier but no initial object; thanks @Jonas Frey),
  • finite Z-sets (elementary topos without a cogenerator)
  • sets with an action of a large free group F(I) (elementary topos without a cogenerating collection).

All properties of the new categories have been decided (which has required a lot of work), with just one exception: I don't know if F(I)-Set is cototal.

The number of missing property combinations goes down from 495 to 321.

I don't think that every one of the remaining 321 combinations (see https://catdat.app/missing) needs to be witnessed by a category, since many of them are not very interesting. But some are: accessible but not well-copowered, Barr-exact but not (all) coequalizers, wide pullbacks but not cosifted limits, split abelian but not well-powered, quasitopos but not Barr-coexact, locally small but not concretizable (because hTop is still not added; and this category is only locally essentially small), locally presentable but not locally ℵ₁-presentable, Grothendieck topos but not finitary algebraic (we already have Sh(X) but need entries for special X to refute finitary algebraicness), finitely accessible but no sifted colimits, exact filtered colimits but no coequalizers, and many more ...

This PR implements a more consistent usage of essentially small collections, i.e. collections that are bijective to a set. This is a more precise version of what is often called a "small set" in the literature (and this wording is now removed from CatDat); it is confusing anyway since the literature often does not distinguish between elements of the universe U and elements of the next universe U+ that are isomorphic to elements in U – both are just called "small sets". But this is not correct, we cannot just ignore the difference (otherwise: what is the category Set?). CatDat now consistently distinguishes between sets (= small collections) and essentially small collections. This is akin to distinguishing between small categories and essentially small categories (namely, the discrete case). The page on foundations has been updated as well.

Martin Brandenburg (Oct 06 2026 at 16:12):

Release v1.22.0 adds various categories of graphs (in the form of sets equipped with certain binary relations, Example) and reduces the number of missing combinations from 321 (last release) to 249. For now, the goal of reducing that number is achieved.

Finally, the two remaining unknown properties of the category of commutative monoids have been decided: it is not cototal and does not have effective cocongruences. In fact, a finitary algebraic category is cototal if and only if it has a cogenerating collection! Kudos to Colin McQuillan and, well, to ChatGPT which found the proof, which yields a smooth transition to the next point.

I have also (finally?) started experimenting with Claude Code, and it is literally a game changer. In particular, I wrote a skill in this PR that is derived from the procedure how I add a new category, which includes deciding its properties and running several scripts, and of course, doing lots of mathematics. I then applied this skill to an empty file for the category of finitely generated groups. With the exception of 3 properties, all of its properties have been decided, and the proofs were already very good. I have then improved them even further.

Another very useful Claude skill that I have created is to proofread and also check the proofs in existing files. Lots of mistakes have been fixed already, but only a couple of files have been checked so far, and I am planning a global run.

Meanwhile, Claude also generated proofs for the 3 remaining properties of finitely generated groups (but this took longer), but I still need to check them, as they are not so easy. Claude also came up with a proof that the category of ω-sets, which Daniel Schepler is currently working on (PR), has no extremal generator. It is clearly much more powerful and makes less mistakes than Gemini (to be precise: Opus 5.5 versus Google Gemini 3.1 Pro), which I have been using before.

But also for the implementation of regular features, or writing up proofs from given sketches, Claude Code is much more convenient than typing out the code or text. I am pretty much late to the game, I know.

Even though all of this is very exciting, it also brings up very fundamental questions, such as "What is mathematics?" ... See also Is AI the End of Math As We Know It?
