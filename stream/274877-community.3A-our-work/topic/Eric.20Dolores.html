Today Ill talk about a paper on Combinatorics that studies the action of the Operad of series parallel posets on order polynomials.

What is category theory doing here? I first need to define Hasse diagrams and the lexicographic sum.

Hasse diagrams: This are graphs where every point of the poset is a node, and we draw edges between an element and their successors. The successors go above the elements.
Then a linear order is a vertical line with nnn nodes. Here in the attached image from Wikipedia user ed g2s, we can find the Hasse diagram of the poset of divisors of 60.

Then the lexicographic sum is defined graphically as follows:
Let PPP be a poset with nnn points (an $$n-$$ary operation) and let Q1,…,QnQ_1,\dots,Q_nQ1​,…,Qn​ be finite posets.
For every iii we take the Hasse diagram of PPP and on the iii node we put the Hasse diagram of QiQ_iQi​, then if there was an edge pip_ipi​ to pjp_jpj​ in the Hasse diagram of PPP, this means the successor of pip_ipi​ is pjp_jpj​. Then in the lexicographic sum we connect all the maximum points of QiQ_iQi​ to the minimum points of QjQ_jQj​.

In the case where P={x1<x2}P=\{x_1<x_2\}P={x1​<x2​} and Q1={q1,q2},Q2={q3}Q_1=\{q_1,q_2\}, Q_2=\{q_3\}Q1​={q1​,q2​},Q2​={q3​} what we obtain is the poset {q1<q3,q2<q3}\{q_1<q_3,q_2<q_3\}{q1​<q3​,q2​<q3​} with Hasse diagram two nodes at the same level, both of them connected to a third node above them.

Here come the operad part:

I work with the operad of finite posets. The operad has as nnn-ary operations posets with nnn points, and the operadic composition
o(P;Q1,…,Qn)=P(Q1,…,Qn)o(P;Q_1,\dots,Q_n)=P(Q_1,\dots,Q_n)o(P;Q1​,…,Qn​)=P(Q1​,…,Qn​)
is the lexicographic sum.

Now in combinatorics people study the following polynomial introduced by Richard Stanley: Ω(P,n)\Omega(P,n)Ω(P,n) which counts the number of "continuous maps/order preserving maps" from PPP to the lineal order with nnn points. Example, Ω(m,x)\Omega(m,x)Ω(m,x) will be the number of maps from a linear order on mmm points to a linear order on xxx points. That is (xm){x\choose m}(mx​) a polynomial on the variable xxx.

And my idea was to consider the set of generating series Z(P)=∑Ω(P,n)xnZ(P)=\sum \Omega(P,n) x^nZ(P)=∑Ω(P,n)xn and define the action of the operad of poset on that set as:

P(Z(Q1),…,Z(Qn))=Z(P(Q1,…,Qn))P(Z(Q_1),\dots,Z(Q_n))=Z(P(Q_1,\dots,Q_n))P(Z(Q1​),…,Z(Qn​))=Z(P(Q1​,…,Qn​)) where P,Q1,…,QnP,Q_1,\dots,Q_nP,Q1​,…,Qn​ are posets.

And then, at least for the posets that are linear orders, or disjoint union of points, or lexicographic sum of this two families, describe explicitly the action.

This sounds abstract and clean, just how category theorist like it, but in combinatorics we prefer explicit calculations. So note that for the poset on two points and no relation between those points {x,y}\{x,y\}{x,y} we have
{x,y}(Z(Q1),Z(Q2))=Z({x,y}(Q1,…,Qn))\{x,y\}(Z(Q_1),Z(Q_2))=Z(\{x,y\}(Q_1,\dots,Q_n)){x,y}(Z(Q1​),Z(Q2​))=Z({x,y}(Q1​,…,Qn​)), now the lexicographic sum with {x,y}\{x,y\}{x,y} is just the disjoint union of posets so we obtain {x,y}(Z(Q1),Z(Q2))=Z(Q1∪Q2)\{x,y\}(Z(Q_1),Z(Q_2))=Z(Q_1\cup Q_2){x,y}(Z(Q1​),Z(Q2​))=Z(Q1​∪Q2​) and by the definition of the polynomial, the number of maps from the disjoint union of posets to a line is the product of the number of maps from each poset to a line. So {x,y}(Z(Q1),Z(Q2))\{x,y\}(Z(Q_1),Z(Q_2)){x,y}(Z(Q1​),Z(Q2​)) is the Hadamard product of the generating series. It takes more effort to describe the action of {x1<x2}\{x_1<x_2\}{x1​<x2​} but you can find it in the paper.

Why people in combinatorics may be interested in the operadic structure? the existence of this structure implies several combinatorial identities and new proofs of old combinatorial identities.

The other thing is, there were several results in combinatorics that were previously isolated results, and we show they are all part of a coherent story, which I think is a more interesting contribution. Now that I remember, there were already papers trying to study operads without saying it.

This is our paper An algebra over the operad of posets and structural binomial identities.

Interacting in another field does require some politics. For example, a common reply by reviewers is that we are just packing definitions. Which is true, but this brings functoriality. So one has to spend time convincing them that (sometimes) this is worth doing.

Eric Dolores (Oct 12 2026 at 21:33):

The Boardman Vogh (B-V) relation identifies both pictures. They represent the same operation.

Now, let's define (informally, graphically) the tensor product of trees $S, T$:

We take the tree T and on every leaf we put a copy of S. Then we apply locally the B-V relation until we reach the tree S but in all the leaves there is a copy of the tree T.

All the possible trees that are formed under this process are elements of the tensor product of S and T. (Formally the tensor product is generated by those diagrams under the relations given by the B-V relation and other natural operadic relations, this is well described on the book).

Here is an example from the paper "Shuffles of trees" by Hoffbeck and Moerijk:

The tree S is a line with two unary vertices, and the tree T is a binary vertex that has one unary vertex on each outgoing edge.

Eric Dolores (Oct 12 2026 at 21:51):

Note that there are different paths that go from the top (left) tree that has S with all leaves replaced by T to the tree that has T with all leaves replaced by S (bottom right).

One of the questions posed by Hoffbeck and Moerdijk was, can we count the number of trees that appear in the tensor product when S is an arbitrary tree and T is a line with nnn-nodes?

And to solve that problem, we studied the generating series ∑#shuffles(S,n)xn\sum \#shuffles(S,n)x^n∑#shuffles(S,n)xn here SSS is the poset of vertices of a tree and #shuffles(S,n)\#shuffles(S,n)#shuffles(S,n) is the number of trees that appear on the tensor product of SSS and the line with nnn nodes.

We showed that those generating series form an algebra over the operad of posets, and we computed explicitly the action of a couple of elements of the operad.

Something I consider interesting was that in our paper some theorems are about structure of the power series, while other theorems have to do with the combinatorial interpretation of the objects. Since we bring ideas from category theory to this field, you can differentiate when a result is from the point of view of the field o when a result is from the point of view of category theory.

I recently meet professor Moerdijk, he is very kind. It is my understanding that most of the questions in his paper "shuffles of trees" are still open in case anyone is interested. I am also open for collaborations!

We were lucky that professor Moerdijk has a lecture on this topic and that the lecture was available online. Otherwise we would have never hear about this problem.

We published our work in the paper Shuffle series.
