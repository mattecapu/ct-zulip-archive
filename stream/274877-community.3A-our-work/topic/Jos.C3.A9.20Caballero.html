Topic: José Caballero

José Caballero (Nov 03 2026 at 16:22):

I am a PhD student in Informatics Engineering at the Universidade do Porto. Before that, I studied Mathematics (Universidad de La Habana, UQÀM and Université de Montréal) and Statistics (Université Laval). I was accredited as an Associated Statistician (A.Stat.) by the Statistical Society of Canada. For more information, see my institutional website.

My current field of research is proof engineering. In particular, I am interested in the formal verification of statistical procedures. This work involves category theory as semantics. One of my inspirations for this research, involving category theory and statistics, is Peter McCullagh's  What is a statistical model? (The Annals of Statistics* 30.5 (2002): 1225-1310.). Right now, I am working on a project related to this paper, but I don't want to talk about it, because I will submit the paper for blind review to a conference.

My favourite proof assistant is Cubical Agda. Unlike some mathematicians who have taken an anti-AI position, I am pro-AI and I am open to collaborating with major tech companies, such as Anthropic and OpenAI. I also like the military applications of AI. Furthermore, I believe that the best strategy for humanity is neither to try to solve the problem of AI alignment nor to oppose the progress of AI. Instead, I advocate for building a society that would be resistant to AI misalignment, e.g., if AI wants to create a lethal virus, it will have no resources to build a lab.

I hope we respect diversity of opinion concerning this last topic. Indeed, I am bound by the Code of Ethics of the Statistical Society of Canada, which includes:

While question and debate are encouraged, criticism should be directed toward procedures rather than persons. Avoid publicly casting doubt on the professional competence of others.

I will treat everybody according to this principle, and I hope that, in return, people criticise my ideas while not assuming that I am a "bad person" just for disagreeing with mainstream opinions.
