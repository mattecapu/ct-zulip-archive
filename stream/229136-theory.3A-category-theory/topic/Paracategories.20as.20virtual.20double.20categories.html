Topic: Paracategories as virtual double categories

philip hackney (Aug 21 2026 at 21:25):

This started as a question but as I wrote it I became more confident and so I suppose it's instead an observation. It seems that Freyd's paracategories are certain kinds of virtual double categories (VDCs). A paracategory is a directed graph equipped with a partially-defined composition operation [−] ⁣:Paths(C)→C[-] \colon Paths(C) \to C[−]:Paths(C)→C satisfying some axioms.

If CCC is a paracategory one can cook up a VDC XXX as follows:

  1. the tight category is discrete on the objects of CCC
  2. the loose morphisms are the edges of CCC
  3. the cells are all of the form f⃗⇒[f⃗]\vec f \Rightarrow [\vec f]f​⇒[f​] (which exist only when the right hand side is defined).

In particular there is at most one cell with a given domain f⃗\vec ff​. Paracategories satisfy an axiom that if [f⃗][\vec f][f​] is defined, then [r⃗,[f⃗],s⃗]≍[r⃗,f⃗,s⃗][\vec r, [\vec f], \vec s] \asymp [\vec r, \vec f, \vec s][r,[f​],s]≍[r,f​,s] (where ≍\asymp≍ is Kleene equality), which implies both the existence of composite cells, and that all cells are opcartesian. There is also a paracategory axiom of the form [f]≍f[f] \asymp f[f]≍f for length one paths, which give the identity cells, and associativity/identity axioms are trivially satisfied since there's at most one cell with a given domain. Since paracategories have identities, the VDC XXX has units for every object.

I believe that among the VDCs, the paracategories are those where the following hold:

  1. the tight category is discrete
  2. every object has a unit
  3. every cell is opcartesian
  4. the category whose morphisms are the unary cells is discrete

Mike Shulman (Aug 22 2026 at 01:34):

I think this isn't really surprising from the monadic point of view on generalized multicategories, where Paths is the free-category monad on directed graphs. A general virtual double category is a directed graph CCC equipped with a span from Paths(C)\mathrm{Paths}(C)Paths(C) to CCC with a composition and identity operations satisfying axioms, and of course a partial function can be regarded as a certain kind of span.
