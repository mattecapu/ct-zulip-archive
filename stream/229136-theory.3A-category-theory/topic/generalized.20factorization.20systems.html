Topic: generalized factorization systems

John Baez (Nov 08 2026 at 10:10):

I'm interested in a kind of a factorization system: a category CCC where we have three classes of morphisms E,M,AE, M, AE,M,A, every morphism ccc in CCC can be factored as c=m∘ec = m \circ ec=m∘e, but the factorization is not unique up to isomorphism, just unique up to morphisms in AAA: if m∘e=m′∘e′m \circ e = m' \circ e'm∘e=m′∘e′ then e′=a∘ee' = a \circ ee′=a∘e and m=m′∘am = m' \circ am=m′∘a for some aaa in AAA. (Draw a diagram.) Various other things are true in my example:

  • E,M,AE,M,AE,M,A are wide subcategories.

  • Isomorphisms are in all of E,M,AE,M,AE,M,A.

  • AAA is naturally a dagger-category.

Manuel Rodríguez (Nov 08 2026 at 12:24):

I think this abstract framework can be instantiated as a decomposition of partial Boolean isomorphisms (Section 4.3.3 of Comfort's thesis), where a factorization system over a subcategory uniquely resolves down to non-invertible, self-adjoint subobjects.

James Deikun (Nov 08 2026 at 12:49):

Actually for this definition to make sense as stated, it's necessary for the intersection of A, M, and E to consist of only isos. And if the "if" is really "iff" then anything in A∩MA \cap MA∩M is in A∩EA \cap EA∩E so the noninvertible morphisms in the three classes must all be disjoint.

I've come across several interesting categories that embed as the full subcategory of cofibrant objects (or a full subcategory of cofibrant objects) of a model category.
