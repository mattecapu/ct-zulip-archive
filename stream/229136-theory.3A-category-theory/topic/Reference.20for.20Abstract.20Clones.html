Eli Hazel (Nov 04 2026 at 08:10):

I'm not completely sure what you mean by abstract clones with multiple colours, but I suspect I don't know of a standard reference for those. Wrt ordinary abstract clones: There is an equivalence of categories between abstract clones and "single-sorted finite product theories", that is, small categories in which all objects are finite products of a single object. This appears in the appendix of:

Taylor, W. Characterizing Mal’cev conditions. Algebra Universalis 3 (1973), 351–397.

The only proper textbook account of abstract clones that I am aware of is Chapter 10 of:

Freese, R. S., McKenzie, R. N., McNulty, G. F., and Taylor, W. F. Algebras, Lattices, Varieties Volume III, 1 ed., vol. 269 of Mathematical Surveys and Monographs. American Mathematical Society, Providence, 2022.

It's aimed at general (universal) algebraists and might be said to be a bit categorist unfriendly---they don't generally allow nullary operations in clones, though they do tell the reader how to include them in Ex. 10.8.16.

Hope this helps :)

Eli Hazel (Nov 05 2026 at 03:13):

In my opinion, the exclusion of nullary operations by algebraists is not sufficient reason to choose a different name for single-sorted clones at least, and is not even universal among algebraists---see Clones with Nullary Operations.

I might be considered biased because of my current work on concrete clones (with nullary operations). If one were to divorce the nullary from non-nullary versions, the term "concrete clone" would lose meaningfulness and a great deal of historical context dating back to the work of Emil Post in the 1920s. I imagine one could also study multi-sorted concrete clones. So I favour the name multi-sorted abstract clones.
