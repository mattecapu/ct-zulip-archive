Zoltan A. Kocsis (Z.A.K.) (Oct 09 2026 at 09:00):

@Ivan Di Liberti One should give a disclaimer here: opting out is useful, but also the wording is seriously misleading and can create a false sense of security.

These opt-outs don't opt out from OpenAI using your content to improve and develop their services and conduct research. Conducting research is of course a maximally broad, and very relevant mandate. All uses that aren't literally training a model (evals, benchmarking, distillation, etc.) remain authorized with the toggle off. The mathematical substance of your sessions can still lawfully flow into OpenAI's internal work through research, evaluation, de-identified reuse, or human review all without violating the policy as written.

Zoltan A. Kocsis (Z.A.K.) (Oct 09 2026 at 09:03):

It's easy to do things that can recreate the pattern observed in the OpenAI release even if they haven't literally trained on the data.

For example, if I build an evaluation set from your sessions and test internal models against it ("I wonder, can our new model improve upon incomplete proofs or partial results written by an older model?"), that's not training on your data. But the resulting proofs will very much be followup work from user content.
