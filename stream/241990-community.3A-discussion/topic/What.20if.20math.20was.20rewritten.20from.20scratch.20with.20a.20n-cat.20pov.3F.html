Topic: What if math was rewritten from scratch with a n-cat pov?

William Oakcraft (Nov 19 2026 at 19:56):

Good afternoon!

I don't know if trying to raise this discussion here, questions or reading & references would be more appropriate, I apologize if it's in the wrong place. This may be feel just like one of those open-ended, speculative, idealist, unpractical and contrarian discussions in mathematics and I also apologize for that but these are personally for me my favorite types of discussions and maybe one of the main reasons I actually love mathematics.

I was studying Polygraphs: From Rewriting to Higher Categories by Ara, Burroni, Guiraud et al a while ago and despite a lot of research this question never truly exited my mind: what if one were to rewrite category theory (its foundations, syntactic presentation - thus logic and mathematics in general) from scratch with absolutely no commitment to any tradition or present-day human intuition about mathematics and foundations but instead seeking a sort of "what if were we to make an entirely new basic education to graduate/PhD level curriculum and thus have the opportunity to truly redo everything from scratch?" commitment to general synthesis, simplicity and pedagogical/ergonomical ease in its most unpragmatical/ahistorical form?

Thus, for instance, I think about going as extreme as having no commitment to our traditional conceptions of equality (and thus isomorphism), morphisms, composition, a Cartesian or monoidal "lambda calculus-like" metatheory (having function application and beta-reduction with implicit eval as central notions), maybe even current functoriality, naturality or more traditional algebraic-style notions, never mind having any hint of respect towards set-theoretical issues or model-dependent formulations of any concepts (thus with also no commitment with it being natural to work with for those coming from more topological backgrounds or not) and I would go as far as having also no respect for Fregean-style (and thus contemporary Martin-Löf style) logic/typing systems, singular unique denoting terms/deterministic functions (contrary to possibly plural and partial terms, non-deterministic and multi-valued functions as standard), the hard division between terms and formulas, objects and concepts etc. (however with equally no hard commitment to more subject-predicate biased paradigms). What you think should be thrown out and under the bus entirely, what should be maintained and what should be pursued if were we to be as idealist and radical as possible? (please bring obscure theses, papers or speculative proposals you think are going in this radically idealist direction if you can).

Of course, one may argue that this is a non-question, as we all have our biases and we would have to start from somewhere. Also, if one changes "category theory", "logic" and even "mathematics" much some would immediately raise the question of whether we are actually talking about category theory (because I would have similarly no commitment to any specific notion of a category). If one is quite immersed into a very specific specialized research program and is very happy with being confined to it, maybe they would have a more "conformist" view that nothing truly ought to be changed (and maybe they find natural that the views of their research program are not reflected in other areas or in the style of foundations/general lingua-franca and basic pre-requisites students should learn before getting to it); but I am not trying to talk to those. I am specifically trying to find the opinions of those who are proud to be a bit opinionated and contrarian about everything: what would you change and what would you promote if you had "absolute power" and no commitment to any tradition and why?

For context: besides my more technical work I also work a lot in mathematical education. I have come to strongly feel that the "calculational/equational/high-school algebraic" style of reasoning (characterized especially by automatic "mindless" normal-form chasing in linear chains of transitive - and desirably also reflexive and symmetric - relations; mostly pointfree/binding-free purely algebraic manipulation of terms; compositional "pattern-match and replace everywhere" definitions; abundance of hyperoperation and inverse/adjoint operation hierarchies that allow "mindless cancelling, simplifying and 'throwing to the other side of the equation'"; a set of simple handwritten/humanly-effective algorithms; and, if possible, a numeral/concrete evaluation/representation object and a forgiving type system - desirably with a "type-free"/single-object system or that feels like it - this may also be considered a main trope of most "numerical"/hard analytic mathematics) is just so ingrained into us due to almost everyone in modern urban societies having gone into almost 2 decades or repeated incessant training into this style of reasoning that as an educator I would feel very advantageous if we could have at least a superficial calculational/"equational"/"numerical-like"/"analytical-feeling" formulation for everything in mathematics and even general science (even if this would need quite an unnatural "jerry-rig/gambiarra" for having this "syntax-sugar" in subjects and underlying content that has nothing of analytical, numerical and maybe not even equational).

Thus, I became increasingly attracted to relational-style mathematics (relation-algebras, allegories, regular categories, profunctorial stuff etc) and compositionally-centered mathematics in general, with a strong aversion to lambda-centered type and rewriting systems and "total, univalued" biased stuff (and thus I am also a bit suspicious and disapproving of any diagrammatic hypes - but especially hostile towards those more "informal/natural language" ones). These are my biases, and in most unhinged days I would probably that if I could throw all mathematical and logical tradition in history that don't fit well with this paradigm in order to have a mathematics that fit better it I happily would (with a bit of exaggeration here, of course). However I am very aware that this is an obsession with what I would argue is the most popular formal reasoning system (although it's a intrinsically historical, traditional and no less arbitrary than any other) in compromise of all others. Some may feel, justifiably, it just wouldn't be worth maintaining (or, as in my case, even increasing) the monopoly of this one formal reasoning system instead of promoting and developing others, and that its intrinsic limitations and biases (towards poset/order stuff/single transitive relations from end to end, no witness tracking of what underlies under those relations/transformations/rewriting steps, structures that are easier to represent in a linear fashion - all the biases coming from algebra and analysis) may actually limit our vision and make us blind to a lot of interesting stuff. As this paradigm is already very prevalent, some may feel it is already probably still making us blind in every area, even in higher category theory environments. If that is the case, what would you think should be "thrown under the bus" instead? What do you think is dispensable and mathematics and what should be focused on instead if we had no compromise to history and tradition?

Of course even in ideal circumstances we are talking about many different motivations here, and they are not necessarily compatible: making the initial learning curve as accessible as possible (as is my objective) may hinder any ability to get outside of the paradigm needed for this. Maybe one would feel that mathematics as is should not become any simpler, more synthetic but that actually this drive for simplicity may just be replacing an unwanted general paradigm for another. In that case, I would also like to hear your suggestions, what you are annoyed by and what you think should be the right path.

PP (Nov 20 2026 at 00:28):

There is always a philosophical motivation driving the development of mathematics. For example, the Pythagorean movement held that everything was governed by numbers. Stephen Wolfram considered everything to be a cellular automaton, and now he has extended his point of view, seeming to see everything as a rewriting system on a hypergraph.

What would be the philosophical motivation for developing mathematics from the nPOV (higher structures point of view)?

Perhaps information theory and the idea of compression? Indeed, nPOV compresses many branches of mathematics into particular cases of the same structure.

Regarding Conceptual Mathematics and other such books (I can remember Eugenia Chang's The Art of Abstraction but there is also a very well-illustrated colored book on arxiv I've seen once but can't remember the name) I love them but my goals would not be just to "teach category theory to children" (which I have some experience giving some category theory classes to high school students) or even to really teach it as a tool for introducing more complex mathematics in a simpler manner (which José Nuno Oliveira's Program Design by Calculation exceeds at - I hear it is used for teaching logic in first-year/freshman courses in computer science, it teaches quite a bit of category theory and type theory - books by Gunther Schmidt in relational mathematics are also great), I feel in this day and age (especially with AI and as there is evidence major specialized textbook publishers are using it) we have even less of an excuse for not being as ideal and radical as possible in our presentation and formulation of any subject. I myself feel obliged to be obsessively perfectionist as such with my own work, to be as synthetic but as general as possible, to aim for computational efficiency as well as comprehension and elegance, to aim at both formal perfection in the content but also aesthetic/notational perfection in the presentation. I feel many of us have such very idealist and radical personal opinions about how foundations, presentation and general mathematical practice should and should not be and I would like to know them, because in this day and age we have each day less of an excuse to be traditionalist, conservative or even "pragmatic".
