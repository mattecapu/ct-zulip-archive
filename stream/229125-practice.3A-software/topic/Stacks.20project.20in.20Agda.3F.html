Isabelle Hol (she/her) (Nov 06 2026 at 16:56):

The primary friction when formalising The Stacks Project in Lean stems from Lean's Uniqueness of Identity Proofs, which restricts equality to strict, 0-truncated equivalence. This strictness causes significant hurdles in higher-dimensional geometric concepts such as categories fibred in groupoids, descent theory, algebraic stacks, and derived categories. In Lean, mathematicians must manually manage canonical isomorphisms and prove extensive coherence diagrams whenever constructing 2-fibre products or gluing schemes along transition isomorphisms. Conversely, Cubical Agda excels in these areas because its Univalent Type Theory natively treats 1-truncated types as groupoids, turning isomorphisms into true identity paths via univalence. Furthermore, Cubical Agda's Higher Inductive Types allow for the direct construction of glued colimits, simplicial spaces, and localisations without combinatorial boilerplate.

Isabelle Hol (she/her) (Nov 06 2026 at 19:25):

The obstruction to representing arbitrary higher categories in plain HoTT is an obstruction to expressing infinite coherence conveniently, not an obstruction that forces higher morphisms to have a bounded truncation level.

Moreover, when that coherence is supplied through two-level type theory or a simplicial framework, univalence remains mathematically significant through completeness, saturation and the identification of equivalence with equality.

@James Deikun , I think your argument may fail to establish either that Cubical Agda is inadequate for general stacks or that Lean has an intrinsic foundational advantage in this area.

It does establish a narrower concern: that native Cubical Agda alone does not currently make arbitrary higher categorical structures as straightforward as ordinary univalent categories.
For choosing a proof assistant to formalise the Stacks Project, I would consequently put much greater weight on the univalent treatment of ordinary categories, bicategories, pseudofunctors and descent than on hypothetical unrestricted higher-category capabilities. These are the structures actually encountered in large portions of the project, and they provide a much more concrete basis for a comparison between Cubical Agda and Lean.

James Deikun (Nov 06 2026 at 19:35):

The coherences can't be supplied through a simplicial framework, and Cubical Agda isn't a two-level type theory. It's not that the coherences can't be expressed "conveniently", they can't be expressed at all. This forces all algebra to be done in a truncated way. In particular, this means you can express 1-categories univalently as having a groupoid of objects, but you cannot express (∞,1)(\infty,1)(∞,1)-categories at all. And the univalent presentation of bicategories, with a 2-groupoid of objects, is in some ways more convenient than the discrete presentation, but it's not any simpler to write out or to construct an example of. In fact, constructing examples can be more complicated because you have to construct the 2-groupoid of objects and equivalences of your bicategory and demonstrate the univalence. The upside is that isomorphisms of univalent bicategories are the same thing as equivalences of univalent bicategories, but this can work against you in some ways because some of the constructs used in the theory of stacks are not invariant under equivalence of categories or bicategories, and then you have to replace these with other constructs that are.
