Topic: Difference between software and hardware

Manuel Rodríguez (Nov 08 2026 at 14:57):

Recently, OpenAI published a video with the slogan hardware is the new software. So, I wonder how to distinguish hardware from software in a rigorous way? Below is my first attempt, that may be wrong, and I would appreciate any correction.

The hardware is defined as a category ℋ representing a physical system, where objects x, y ∈ Ob(ℋ) are the possible states and the morphisms f ∈ Hom(x, y) are the transitions between them.

The software is defined as the syntactic category 𝒮, a formal blueprint where objects are abstract data types and morphisms are programmatic functions.

The compiler is defined as a structure-preserving functor F: 𝒮 → ℋ, representing the active translation mechanism that grounds the abstract software into physical reality by systematically mapping data types to hardware states and functions to physical transitions.
